Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026 (using your levels on another device)

The short version

  • No account. No email, no password, no sign-in. You pick a character, Alex or Sara, so the story can greet you; the choice stays on your devices.
  • No ads, no third-party analytics, no tracking across apps or websites.
  • Your learning progress lives on your devices. On iPhone, iPad and Mac it syncs through your own iCloud account; on Android it can be included in your device’s own backup. We can’t see it.
  • The microphone is used only for speaking exercises, and only if you allow it.
  • Reminders are created on your device. Nothing about them is sent to us.
  • To use levels you bought on your other devices, iPhone or Android, the app keeps a record of the purchases under a random ID, with RevenueCat and on our own small server. No name, email or store account is part of it. How it works.

Who we are

El Recreo is an app for learning Rioplatense Spanish on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android, made by an independent developer, Aleksandr Riakhin (“we”, “us”). This policy covers the El Recreo app and this website. If you have a question about it, write to support@alexriakhin.com.

What we don’t collect

El Recreo is built so that we don’t need your personal data. The app has no account system and no sign-up. It contains no advertising, no third-party analytics and no tracking SDKs, and it does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, purchases go through the App Store and progress sync goes through your own iCloud account, both run by Apple. On Android, purchases go through Google Play, run by Google. The only other services the app talks to are the ones that let you use your levels on another device: RevenueCat and our own small link server, described below. We don’t sell personal data or share it for advertising.

Your learning progress

Lessons you’ve completed, your answers, your settings and similar progress data are stored on your device. If you’re signed in to iCloud, this data is synced between your devices through Apple’s iCloud (CloudKit), in the private database of your iCloud account. Data in that private database is accessible to you, not to us; Apple handles it under the Apple Privacy Policy.

To remove it, delete the app from your devices. To also remove the synced copy, go to Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage and delete El Recreo’s data.

On Android, progress and settings are stored on your device. If Android backup is turned on for your Google account, Android may include them in that backup and restore them on a new phone; Google handles that backup under the Google Privacy Policy, and we can’t see it. To remove the data, uninstall the app; you can manage backups in your device’s Settings → Google → Backup.

Your character

When you first open the app, you pick who you are in the story: Alex or Sara. The choice sets the name the characters call you and the voice your own lines are spoken in. You never type a name, and you can change the character any time in Settings. It is stored on your device and, if you’re signed in to iCloud, on Apple devices synced to your other devices through iCloud key-value storage in your iCloud account. We never receive it.

Reminders and notifications

If you turn on reminders, the app schedules them on your device; they are not sent from a server. Each reminder is a short note from one of the app’s characters, with their picture, created and shown entirely on your device. Nothing about it is sent to us. You can turn reminders off in the app or in your device’s notification settings.

Widgets and ratings

Home Screen widgets read your progress from a shared container on the same device. Now and then the app may ask you to rate it, using the system’s standard rating prompt (Apple’s on Apple devices, Google Play’s on Android); Apple or Google handles your rating or review, and we only see what’s published in the store.

Problem reports

If you tap “Report a problem” in the app, your own email app opens with a message addressed to us. It already includes where you are in the course (lesson and step), the last Spanish line the app played, the app version, your device model, its system version and the app’s language, so we can find and fix the problem. Nothing is sent unless you send that email yourself, and we use it only to reply and fix the issue.

Microphone and speech recognition

Some exercises let you practice speaking. They are optional. The first time you start one, your device asks for permission to use the microphone (and, on Apple devices, speech recognition); you can say no and still use the rest of the app, and you can change your mind any time in Settings.

When you allow it, your speech is turned into text with the speech recognition built into your device (Apple’s on iPhone, iPad and Mac, Android’s on Android) so the app can check your answer. Recognition runs on your device wherever your device and language support it; otherwise Apple or Google, or the speech service your Android device uses, may process the audio under their own privacy policy. El Recreo does not record, keep or upload your voice, and we never receive it.

Listening to Spanish

The Spanish you hear in the app is recorded in advance. The first level’s recordings are included in the app; those for later levels download from the App Store or Google Play when you get the level, and are then kept on your device. Either way they play offline, and nothing is sent anywhere when you listen. For anything without a recording, the app falls back to the text-to-speech voices built into your device. Either way, nothing is sent to us.

Purchases

Parts of the course are sold as one-time in-app purchases. Payment is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and by Google through Google Play on Android; we never receive your payment details, your Apple ID or your Google account.

To check which levels you own and to restore them on your other devices with the same store, the app asks the store directly: the App Store through Apple’s StoreKit, or Google Play through Google Play Billing. Apple and Google keep the purchase records. We also see the aggregate sales information shown in App Store Connect and Google Play Console, which doesn’t identify you.

Using your levels on another device

A level you buy can also be used on your other devices, on either store: for example, buy on an iPhone and use it on an Android tablet. Up to five devices share one set of levels (a “group”). There is still no account: devices join a group with a one-time code that one of them shows. Your learning progress is never part of this; each device keeps its own.

RevenueCat. To know which levels a group owns on both stores, the app uses RevenueCat, a company that processes in-app purchase records on our behalf. When the app starts it connects to RevenueCat with a random ID the app made (it looks like er_3f9a…). On iPhone, iPad and Mac that ID is shared by your Apple devices through iCloud key-value storage in your iCloud account; on Android, the app derives it from a scrambled (hashed) form of your first purchase’s record, so your Android devices on one Google account get the same ID. RevenueCat receives that ID, the records of the purchases made in the app (which levels, when, the price and currency, the store and its country, and the store’s transaction identifiers or receipts), and technical details such as the app and system version, the device type, the language and the IP address of the connection. It does not receive your name, email, Apple ID or Google account, and El Recreo doesn’t send it any advertising identifier. RevenueCat handles this data under its Privacy Policy.

Our link server. The codes and the list of devices are kept by a small server of ours, run on Cloudflare. It stores, per group: the group’s random ID, and for each device a random ID made by the app when it was installed, the device model (such as “iPhone 16” or “Pixel 9 Pro”), whether it’s iPhone/iPad/Mac or Android, whether it bought levels itself or joined with a code, and when it was added and last opened the app. A code is kept for 10 minutes or until it’s used. To stop people guessing codes, the server counts attempts per group and per IP address; those counters are deleted after two hours. Only devices that have levels, or that enter a code, talk to this server. Cloudflare may keep short-lived request logs, including IP addresses, to run and secure the service (see the Cloudflare Privacy Policy).

How long, and how to remove it. A device that isn’t opened for 60 days drops off its group’s list, and a group’s list is deleted two years after its last activity. You can take a device out of a group any time: on that device, Settings › Use on another device › Stop using these levels here; from the device that bought the levels, tap Remove next to another device. To have a group’s list and its RevenueCat record deleted, write to support@alexriakhin.com with a code from one of its devices (Settings › Use on another device › Show a code), so we can find the group; we delete it within 30 days. Deleting these records doesn’t affect what you bought: the App Store or Google Play still gives your levels to devices on the same account.

This website

This website is a set of static pages hosted on GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking scripts. The voice samples on the home page are audio files served from this same site, and they play only when you tap them. Like any web host, GitHub may log technical data such as IP addresses to keep the service secure (see the GitHub Privacy Statement). The site’s fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, so your browser connects to Google’s servers to download them (see the Google Privacy Policy). If you email us, we use your message and address only to reply.

Children

El Recreo doesn’t ask anyone for personal information, including children. The only data that leaves a device is described above: progress in your own iCloud account or Android backup, and, for devices that use levels from a purchase, the random IDs, device details and purchase records used to share levels between your devices.

Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects your privacy, we’ll update this page and the date at the top before the change takes effect.
